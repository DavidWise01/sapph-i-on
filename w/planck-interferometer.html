right method, real experiment

this is the Fermilab Holometer: paired laser interferometers hunting Planck-scale jitter. it can\u2019t resolve a point (nothing can) — but the correlated signal of two cameras can reveal the foam\u2019s wobble. you get the tremble, not the picture.

🧒 toddler corner — two microphones at the bottom of the sea

you can\u2019t take a photo of the very bottom of the ocean — it\u2019s too dark and too churny, and any flashbulb bright enough would boil the water. so pointing a super-precise laser pointer down there is hopeless: aim it fine enough and the light itself burns a hole. BUT — put two microphones down there instead and just listen. you\u2019ll never see the bottom, but if it\u2019s trembling, both mics hear the same shiver at the same time, and that match tells you the deep is alive and shaking — even though you never saw it. that\u2019s the trick: at the bottom of everything you can\u2019t look, but you can listen for the shiver.

Two cameras and a 0.001 degree laser at the Planck floor: interferometry that cannot resolve below the Planck length, because the baseline is sub-Planck, the angular tick lands at ten to the minus forty meters, and the beam photon exceeds Planck energy. But like the Holometer, it can detect the foam's statistical jitter.
