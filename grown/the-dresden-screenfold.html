The Dresden Screenfold

A screenfold of bark paper (amate), read in red and black like the Codex Dresdensis, the oldest surviving Maya book. Swipe the leaves sideways. Each leaf works: a bar-and-dot calculator, Dave's typed cipher read as Maya numbers, the real Venus table, the real eclipse-warning intervals, and the calendar-round wheel. Every change is recorded on Dave's DurableAnchor ledger below. Labels: LIT sourced fact · MODEL computed here · READING an interpretation, not a decipherment.

I · Bar, dot, shell

LITMaya numerals are written with a dot for 1, a bar for 5 and a shell for 0, stacked in places of 20 (bottom place first). Calendar counts use 18 in the third place (1 tun = 360 days).

II · Dave's cipher

READINGType dots . and bars |; spaces separate glyphs. Each group is read as one Maya numeral. A valid glyph has at most 4 dots and 3 bars (0–19).

III · Venus table

LITDresden pp. 24 and 46–50. Each Venus round of 584 days is split 236 (morning star) + 90 (gone) + 250 (evening star) + 8 (gone). Five rounds fill 2920 days = 8 haab years; the table runs 13 times: 37,960 days = 146 tzolk'in = 104 years.

IV · Eclipse warnings

LITDresden pp. 51–58: 69 stations over 405 lunations, 11,960 days. Most steps are 177 days (6 moons, written 8.17), a few are 148 (5 moons, written 7.8) before each of the 10 eclipse pictures; some 177s are counted as 178 (7 in the column summary used here, giving 11,959; the stated span is 11,960, and the literature discusses both 11,959- and 11,960-day versions). Step through and the station lights when an eclipse window is warned.

V · Calendar round

LITThe tzolk'in (13 numbers × 20 day names = 260) and haab (18 months × 20 + 5 Wayeb = 365) mesh like gears; they realign every 18,980 days (52 years). MODELDates use the GMT correlation 584283; creation day 4 Ajaw 8 Kumk'u.
