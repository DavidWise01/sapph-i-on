An order-7 cube holds 756 windows of two by two.

LIT Three orientations each keep 7 layers of 6 by 6 windows, which is 252. Three times 252 is 756. The same total is 3 times 343, minus 6 times 49, plus 3 times 7. The terms from n = 0 through n = 8 match that formula, and the order-4 recurrence carries 0, 0, 6, 36 forward to 756 and then to 1176. Six times 126 is 756. A 7 by 7 by 7 lattice of unit cubes has 3 times 49 times 8 faces, which is 1176. Origins from −1 through 6 make 448 tries on one orientation, and 196 of them fall off the cube. Three times 196 is 588. The keeper at (3,3,3) sits in 12 windows. The corner (0,0,0) sits in 3. A second run matches the first. A failed count throws.

AMBER OEIS A270205, read on 2026-10-28, counts the 2 by 2 planar subsets of an n by n by n cube. Craig Knecht entered the sequence on 2016-03-13. The formula printed there is 3n cubed minus 6n squared plus 3n, and the listed term a(7) is 756. The same page says a(n) equals 6 times A002411(n−1), and the A002411 title there is the pentagonal pyramidal number n squared times (n+1) over 2. A comment by Mohammed Yaseen says a(n+1) is the number of unit faces required to make an n by n by n cubic lattice. WALL The 756 count is geometric. It does not place the numbers 1 through 343, and it does not show that a most-perfect cube of order 7 exists. The note on that OEIS page about 64 subcubes summing to 260 belongs to an order-4 example, not to this cube. The unit-face count 1176 is the lattice of unit cubes, not a magic line.
