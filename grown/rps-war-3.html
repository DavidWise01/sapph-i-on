How it ends up
One game: the side the RPS winner sends first wins, in 31 moves, from 1832 to 1862. RPS is played once, at the start. Its winner takes a seat and picks who goes first. The war is then move-based until one civilization reaches 0. With one move per year, it ends in 1862. Moving first decides it: P1-first gives P1 the win in 31 moves, and P2-first gives P2 the win in 31 moves. The first-mover effect is 100%. If the RPS winner seats itself as a fighter and moves first, it wins.


“1 million”, both ways. 1,000,000 moves: the war never gets past move 31, so there is nothing more to run. 1,000,000 repeated games, each with its own RPS: RPS was won by X1 0.3335, X2 0.3341, X3 0.3324 (mean 2.25 throws, max 15), and every war went to whoever moved first. With the RPS winner choosing P1 and itself first, it wins every game, so the war winners are also about 1/3 each.
