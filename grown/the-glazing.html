The Glazing

Chapter 4: the carving. To rise, Kessa has to pass as Frustulate. Over nights behind the lamp-screen, a Violet glass-wright named Ombrel glazes her. He lays a shell of silica plates over her body, and each plate needs a sworn oath from her. The puppet is jointed and hangs from rods. Its legs are a chain of segments that follow the rod by a simple reach rule (inverse kinematics), so the gait emerges from the chain. Each plate added hides one more leg, until the shadow on the screen reads as a diatom. Drag the rod to move her. Each plate is an oath reserved on the ledger. Swearing a different oath for the same plate is refused, because an oath once given can't be taken back.

LIT: diatoms have two overlapping silica valves, while tardigrades have a cuticle and eight legs with claws. INVENTED: the glazing, Kessa and Ombrel. MODEL: FABRIK-style follow-the-leader joint chains. ANALOGY: wayang-style shadow play, with a lamp, a screen and rods.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
