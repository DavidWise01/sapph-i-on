LITBasaltic lava erupts at roughly 1,100–1,200 °C (≈1,370–1,470 K) and glows orange-yellow; as a crust cools below about 600 °C its visible glow fades to dull red and then black, while it keeps radiating in the infrared. (The temperature range is a commonly quoted value; I didn't fetch a primary volcanological source this run.)

MODELA cooling sheet: each cell loses heat by radiation (∝ T⁴, Stefan–Boltzmann, relative units) and diffusion. Cracks reopen at random and expose hot melt; the colour of each cell is computed from Planck's law. −Orange+: the cooling crust is the minus side, fresh melt the plus side, and the boundary between them shines brightest.
