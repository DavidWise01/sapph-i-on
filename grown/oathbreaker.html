OATHBREAKER

Chapter 7: the betrayal. After the war game the houses have to keep making pacts, round after round. Each house follows one strategy: cooperate always, defect always, tit-for-tat, grudge or random. Each round, every house plays every other house once, and every pledge is an oath reserved on the ledger. Pennate's Electrum heir, Aurelith, tries to break an oath after giving it, so that the pledge reads defect where it had read cooperate. The ledger refuses that as a double vote. Here a betrayal can't be hidden, only done openly next round. Between rounds, the worst house copies the best house's strategy, so strategies evolve. The op-art field is two line gratings, one for cooperation and one for defection. Their moiré shows the balance of trust. Tap to have Aurelith try the hidden betrayal.

LIT: in Axelrod's tournaments (1980) tit-for-tat did well, and imitation dynamics spread successful strategies. Op art (Riley, Vasarely, 1960s) used moiré and interference. INVENTED: the houses and Aurelith. MODEL: an iterated prisoner's dilemma with payoffs T 5, R 3, P 1, S 0.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
