The Ionization Ladder

How hard is it to pull one electron off an atom? 108 measured answers, one rhythm, seven rungs.

1 · The question

The first ionization energy is the energy needed to strip the outermost electron from a neutral atom in the gas phase. It is measured in electronvolts (eV). Hydrogen needs 13.6 eV.

2 · The peaks

Plot all of them against atomic number and the noble gases stand at the top of every period: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn. A closed shell holds its electrons hardest. Helium, at 24.6 eV, holds tightest of all.

3 · The cliffs

One step past each peak, the energy collapses. The alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr) carry one electron alone in a new shell, far from the nucleus and shielded by everything beneath. Caesium gives it up for about 3.9 eV.

4 · The flat middle

Across the transition metals and the lanthanides the line barely moves. The electrons being added are going into inner d and f shells, so the outermost one feels almost the same pull each step.

5 · The ladder

Seven periods, seven rungs: the same climb and fall, again and again. This rhythm is the shell structure that the element spheres draw as rings. Past Z ≈ 108 there are no measurements: the atoms live for seconds or less, a few at a time.
