OUTWARD

Chapter 10: on the wind. The clean moss is real, but small, and the blight will come. So the two peoples do what both their kinds have always done: they let the wind carry them. Tuns and diatom cells are light enough to ride dust. The council of both peoples votes on the launch. Each delegate's vote is a reservation on the ledger, and no one can vote twice. Each passenger then rides a wind field of gusts and eddies, with no steering. A new colony takes root only where a tun and a diatom land close together, because the mutualism of book I still holds. Where those colonies end up emerges from the wind alone. The patch is stitched as each colony is founded. Tap to launch another flight.

LIT: tardigrades (as tuns) and diatoms disperse by wind, and microscopic life is found in high-altitude air. Embroidered mission patches date from the 1960s. MODEL: particles in a curl-noise wind with gravity settling. INVENTED: the vote, the flight and the colonies. This ends Book II.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
