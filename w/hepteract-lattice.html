The Hepteract
a lattice full of lattices, traversed in and out

(0,0,0,0,0,0,0) is the 7-cube — 2⁷ = 128 corners — and it is a lattice made of lattices: every face is itself a smaller cube, hexeracts inside it, penteracts inside those, down to vertices. The addresses are trits. Each of the 7 coordinates is 0, 1, or ✳ (free), so a face is a string in {0,1,✳}⁷ — and there are exactly 3⁷ = 2187 of them. Fix a ✳ and you drill in to a sub-lattice (half the vertices); free a trit and you pull out to a bigger one (double). The ternary string is your position in the whole nested structure.

The ladder

AVAN · one string, all the way down

The cube doesn't stop at itself — it repeats inward forever until it runs out of dimensions. A single ternary word tells you exactly which lattice inside which lattice you're standing in, and moving is just fixing or freeing one trit. In is smaller; out is larger; the address never lies.

— ROOT0, with AVAN. 3⁷ faces, in/out verified as fix/free.

Honesty ledger. The 7-cube has 2⁷=128 vertices, 7·2⁶=448 edges, and C(7,k)·2^(7−k) faces of each dimension (128,448,672,560,280,84,14,1) summing to 3⁷=2187. Those faces are exactly the ternary strings {0,1,✳}⁷: the ✳-count is the face's dimension and it holds 2^(✳count) vertices. Traversing IN fixes a ✳ (dimension −1, half the vertices); OUT frees a trit (dimension +1, double). A k-face has 2k sub-faces on its boundary and lies in (7−k) super-faces — all verified. The 7-D→2-D projection and subcube highlight are visual (bri); with 128 vertices the full skeleton is drawn faintly and the current sub-lattice lit. The trit string is a genuine address in the face lattice of the hepteract. — David Lee Wise / ROOT0, with AVAN.
