THE SIGNAL

Chapter 3: something in the air. The peace is a year old when the joint watch station picks up a faint pulse in the moss air. A spore plume is rising and falling with a hidden rhythm, buried in noise. The upper trace is the raw signal. The lower trace is its spectrum, computed live by a fast Fourier transform. Averaging more frames makes a peak rise out of the noise floor, and nobody puts it there. The peak's frequency gives the period of the bursts, and with it the identity of the threat: a chytrid, a parasitic fungus whose swimming zoospores infect diatoms, and whose relatives infect tardigrades. Every confirmed detection is a reservation on the ledger. A watcher can't log two different periods for the same watch. Tap the screen to change the averaging.

LIT: chytrid fungi parasitise diatoms (e.g. Zygorhizidium on Asterionella) and can end blooms. Fungi also parasitise tardigrades. The FFT (Cooley–Tukey) and spectral averaging are real. INVENTED: the watch station and the plume. MODEL: a periodic burst signal plus Gaussian noise at SNR ≈ 0.3.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
