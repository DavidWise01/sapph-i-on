THE MINT IN THE TOWER

LITNewton became Warden of the Royal Mint in 1696 and Master in 1699, in the middle of the Great Recoinage of 1696–99, when England's worn and clipped hammered silver was called in and re-struck as machine-milled coin whose lettered or grained edges showed any clipping. (Royal Mint; Oxford "Newton and the Mint" project.)

MODELA market of agents spending silver coins. Some clip a sliver from full coins before passing them on; everyone prefers to pay with light coins and keep heavy ones (Gresham's law: bad money drives out good). Watch the circulating weight sink. Then recoin: milled coins can't be clipped unnoticed. Every coin is struck once on Dave's ledger; presenting the same coin to be struck twice is refused as a double vote.
