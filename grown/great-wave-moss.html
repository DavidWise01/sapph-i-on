The Great Wave off the Moss (苔沖浪裏)

The water is a one-dimensional wave equation. It is driven from the left, slowed in the shallows, and steepened by a nonlinear term. Wherever the slope passes a threshold the crest breaks and grows foam claws, which are small self-similar curls that split until they are too small to cut. Patricia rides in a tun-boat, and a mountain of sand grains and diatoms stands in for Fuji. Every breaking crest is a reservation on the ledger. Tap the sea to drop a stone.

LIT: Hokusai's Great Wave off Kanagawa (c. 1831) used imported Prussian blue, the same pigment as a cyanotype. Tardigrades survive desiccation as tuns. MODEL: the wave is u_tt = c²u_xx − damping, with a steepening term. ANALOGY: the foam claws come from a recursive curl rule, not from fluid dynamics.
