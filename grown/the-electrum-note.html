The Electrum Note

This is a banknote whose security printing comes from the ledger itself. Each note is issued as a reservation: the serial is the epoch, and the head of Dave's kernel at issue time is the plate. The guilloché rosettes are sums of rotating circles (hypotrochoids), and their radii and speeds are read from the ledger head, so no two notes share a pattern. Tap the note to counterfeit it: one rosette parameter is nudged. The note no longer hashes to its issued head, and the ledger refuses it.

LIT: guilloché is engine-turned line work (the rose engine, 16th c. onward), still used on banknotes because it is hard to copy. Electrum was the metal of the first coins (Lydia, c. 600 BCE). MODEL: each rosette is z(t) = Σ rₖ e^{i ωₖ t}. ANALOGY: the note is drawn with the electrum 79/21 palette, gold for 79% of the strokes and silver for 21%.
