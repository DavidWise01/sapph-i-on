The Book of Hours of 720

The book's clock turns 720°, so a full cycle is two days of 24 hours, which makes 48 hours in two columns on one folio. The hour you are in now (local time) is rubricated and gilded. Vines of rinceaux grow along the margins by one rule: a tip curls toward the page edge and branches at random, putting out a leaf at each fork. Drolleries (Patricia, a diatom, an electrum ring) settle where the vines curl tightest. Each hour that passes is a reservation on the ledger, with epoch = (cycles since 1970) × 48 + hour + 1, so the ledger refuses any attempt to turn the clock back. Tap the margin to plant a vine.

LIT: books of hours (13th–16th c.) organised prayer by canonical hours, and rinceaux and drolleries filled their margins. Gold leaf was laid over a raised gesso ground. INVENTED: the 720° hours and the drolleries. ANALOGY: the gilding is a gradient, not metal.
