The Tun Survey · Sheet 1:20 (moss cushion, quadrangle 0)

A survey of the water film on a moss cushion, mapped the way a topographic quadrangle maps a mountain. The contours are cut by marching squares. Water spreads by diffusion, evaporates faster on the high ground and pools in the hollows. Each tardigrade follows one rule: walk downhill toward water, and curl into a tun (▲, a survey triangle) wherever the film is too thin. Patrols and tun-states change as the land dries. Tap the map to rain on it. Each survey revision is a reservation on the ledger.

LIT: tardigrades need a water film to be active. When it dries they enter anhydrobiosis as a tun, and they revive when water returns. Marching squares (Lorensen & Cline, 1987, as marching cubes) traces iso-lines. MODEL: diffusion + evaporation ∝ elevation, on a 100×70 grid. ANALOGY: survey symbols, scale and quadrangle naming.
