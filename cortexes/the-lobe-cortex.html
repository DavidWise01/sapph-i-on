The bridge and the centre.

The colour line cannot close by itself. Red and violet sit at the ends of a line: red at 700 nm, violet at 380 nm. Mixed together they make purples, which no single wavelength of light can produce. The 7.5 bridge is that straight run of mixed colour, the chord that closes the loop. At the centre of the loop is 8.0, the white point where all the colours add up to white. This is the lobe: the part of the cortex that holds the bridge and the centre.

The cortex

The lobe cortex holds the bridge at 7.5 and the centre at 8.0. On the bridge, you are walking between red and violet. At 7.5 you are exactly halfway along — not halfway from root to crown, but halfway from 7 to 8. That is the seam.

The 8.0 centre is where the loop closes. When red and violet touch at 7.5 and turn into each other, the colour line becomes a ring again — red feeding crown through the bridge, and the white point at the centre.

In small words: you cannot make purple by mixing red and violet into a single spot of light. But you can walk along a straight line from red to violet and back. The bridge is that line. The centre is where the line meets white.
