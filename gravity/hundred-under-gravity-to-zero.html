how the 100 become 0. in ordinary matter each element is set by its protons, Z. pack matter tight enough and the electrons are forced into the nuclei: a proton plus an electron makes a neutron, and a neutrino flies out. nuclei turn neutron-rich, then above about 4 × 10¹⁴ kg/m³ neutrons start dripping out of them. at nuclear density, 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³, the nuclei dissolve into one neutron fluid. every element is gone; the page calls that element 0. the steps between are a smooth sketch (AMBER); the thresholds are LIT. a few percent of protons and electrons always stay, because pure neutrons would decay, so "= 0" is really ≈ 0.07. the clock. a star's iron core falls in within a few tenths of a second: at 10¹³ kg/m³ the free-fall time is 0.02 s. it bounces at nuclear density, and the new neutron star then pours out neutrinos for about 10 seconds; in 1987 detectors caught that flash, lasting about 12 seconds. your 3 + 6 = 9 s lands inside it. the Planck line. 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m is the Planck length, where gravity itself turns quantum. three of them, turned into time at light speed, is 1.6 × 10⁻⁴³ s. added to 6 seconds, that's a part in 10⁴⁴: zero to any clock ever built, but not zero.
small words: squeeze anything hard enough and it all turns into the same stuff, neutrons, like a neutron star. gold, iron, carbon: all gone, all the same. it happens fast, in less than a second, and then the star glows with invisible little particles for about 10 seconds.
