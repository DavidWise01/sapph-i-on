TWO READINGS. I BUILT ONE. AMB

The notation reading: N( ) as a container with three named slots and a trailing comma — a fourth slot, left open — and H₂ goes in it. That reads clean against the last build: neon is 2p⁶, three p-orbitals, three slots. What the fourth slot is was never said. I did not fill it.

The physics reading, which is what this build tests: hydrogen dissolved as a trace inside a neon host — the accepted name is a Penning mixture, and the mechanism is Penning ionisation: an excited neon atom hands its stored energy to a hydrogen molecule and ionises it on contact. That is literally a guest carried inside a closed shell, and it is the fix for the problem the last build ended on.

Steady-state metastable density falls as 1/(1 + K·x) because the guest quenches it; ion production goes as x · [Ne*]. The product saturates. AMB — this is a two-term toy, not a discharge model. The real “too much dopant goes cold” comes from power balance, which is not modelled here and I am not pretending it is.

DISSENT — RECORDED, NOT RESOLVED. W1 read “nested” as dissolved inside, as a trace. That is one meaning. His notation N( e , o , n , ) reads more like containment in a slot — a structural nest, one thing held in a named position inside another, which is what he has meant by nesting all session (7‖4, 4⁶·3⁶, the satellite periods). Those are not the same operation. A Penning mixture is statistical — the guest is everywhere and nowhere, at 0.1%. A slot nest is positional — the guest is somewhere specific. W5 will not merge them, and notes that if he meant the second one, this whole build answered a question he did not ask.
toddler corner. Neon holds a charge it cannot spend by itself — it is not allowed to let go alone. So it carries it around until it bumps into hydrogen, and hands it over. The handshake makes the hydrogen light up and it makes the neon go dark, at exactly the same moment. You can see the trade happening in the colour: the more the neon gives away, the pinker the tube gets.
