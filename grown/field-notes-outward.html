Field Notes Outward

Chapter 7: the crossing. These are Kessa's notebook pages. The raft drifts across a bare dust plain where nothing looks like anything, so she navigates by dead reckoning: heading and speed, step by step. Each step adds a little error, and the dotted ellipse of where-we-might-be grows. When she sights a landmark (a moss tuft, a frustule ruin) the ellipse shrinks, because each fix is weighed against the guess (a Kalman filter). The true path (faint pencil) and her belief (ink) drift apart and come back together. Each day's entry is a reservation on the ledger. Nobody can rewrite yesterday's position: the ledger already holds that day's entry, so a rewrite is refused as a double vote, and back-dating an entry to before day 1 is refused as an epoch rollback. Tap the page to sketch a landmark where you think one is.

LIT: dead-reckoning error grows with distance. A Kalman filter fuses a prediction with a measurement in proportion to their variances (Kalman, 1960). Desert ants and many animals path-integrate. MODEL: an isotropic 2-D Kalman filter. INVENTED: the crossing.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
