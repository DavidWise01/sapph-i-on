Chapter 10: the charter. Sixteen delegates sit in the first joint assembly: eight from the Frustulate and eight from the Tunborn, one for each caste colour on each side. They vote on four articles by ranked ballot, and the ballots are counted by instant runoff until one option has a majority. Each delegate starts with their faction's preference and moves toward the median as debate rounds pass (opinion averaging with stubbornness). Whether a charter passes, and what it says, emerges from that. Every ballot is a reservation on the ledger: (delegate, article). A delegate who tries to vote twice is refused, and the paper prints that too. Tap the front page to run another debate round.

LIT: instant-runoff voting and DeGroot-style opinion averaging are real methods. INVENTED: the assembly, its articles and the end of the story. ANALOGY: a halftone broadsheet front page.

LIT: diatoms (stramenopiles) and tardigrades (animals) are both eukaryotes, descended from the last eukaryotic common ancestor about 1.8 to 2 billion years ago. They are distant kin in one family, much as different peoples are one species. Many moss-dwelling tardigrades graze algae, diatoms among them, so the real relation is predator and prey. INVENTED: their castes, houses, war and politics. Story inspired by Red Rising by Pierce Brown. All names, characters and text here are original.
